    03

    How Claude designed it

    1. 01

      Model the whole power chain

      Battery sag, ESC, motor KV, resistance and no-load current set the rpm the prop can actually reach. Thrust is scored at that equilibrium, not at a fixed rpm.

    2. 02

      Optimize over the race envelope

      Blade-element momentum theory at 0, 43, 86, 130 and 162 km/h, weighted toward speed, with a 50 A current cap and a hover-efficiency term. About 430 candidate props per run.

    3. 03

      Build and check it in Fusion

      18 airfoil sections lofted, patterned three times and joined to the hub in 12 seconds. Volumes are checked against the analytic values and the root is kept 0.3 mm clear of the motor bell.

    04

    Read this before you fly it

    • Part of the gain is current.
    • The optimizer is pressing on its limits. Chord (15 mm) and camber (6 %) both sit on their upper bounds, so the low-Reynolds-number airfoil model may be optimistic. A thrust-stand test against a stock prop is the next step.
    • Axial flow only. The model ignores the edgewise flow a tilted race quad sees in forward flight.
    • The airflow in the 3D view is illustrative. It comes from actuator-disk momentum theory on the modeled blade loading at 86 km/h (inflow, wake contraction, swirl and tip-vortex helices), not from CFD.
